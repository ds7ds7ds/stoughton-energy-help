How the numbers work
Regular National Grid price: 39.5¢/kWh = supply 17.2 + distribution 10.4 + transmission 5.5 + other charges 6.4. Heat Pump Rate, Nov 1–Apr 30, on all your electricity: distribution 4.2¢ lower, and from Nov 1, 2026 transmission about 3.5¢ lower. Winter price 31.8¢.

  • Heat pump COPs come from an hour-by-hour model of a typical Norwood year: COP and capacity drop below freezing, the unit runs as an air conditioner a few minutes an hour to defrost (about 173 hours a year), and strip heat covers defrost and the coldest hours. Field studies agree: about 2.0 for ducted systems in Massachusetts.
  • If you type your bills, the page works backwards from them. Oil or propane: dollars ÷ price = gallons, minus hot water (about 144 gallons of oil or 219 of propane a year). Gas: (bill − $12.20) × 12 ÷ $2.60 = therms, minus 200 for hot water. Electric: (bill − $10) × 12 ÷ 39.5¢ = kWh, minus cooling (and minus heat and hot water in electric-heat homes). Gasoline: dollars ÷ $/gallon × mpg = miles.
  • No bills typed: house size sets the heat, small 40, medium 64, large 90 million BTU a year (medium = 800 therms of gas or 560 gallons of oil), and other electricity is 600 kWh a month.
  • Insulation cuts heating 15%; Mass Save pays about 90% of it (100% for income-eligible homes). Hot water 12 million BTU a year; old tank 60% (fuel) or 92% (electric); heat pump water heater COP 2.5. Washer-dryer saves 350 kWh a year; old fridge pickup 700 kWh.
  • Solar 1,200 kWh per kW a year, 60% in May–Oct. Solar you use saves your full price; extra becomes credits worth about 33¢; credits can't pay the $10 monthly connection fee, so a net-zero house still pays that. SMART about 3¢ per kWh. Battery: ConnectedSolutions about $1,100 a year. Geothermal: $65,000–75,000 installed with drilling (we use $70,000), season COP 3.2.
  • Loans: HEAT Loan 0%, up to $25,000, 7 years, for insulation, heat pumps, heat pump water heaters and batteries; apply before the work is installed. Energy Saver Home Loan 0.5% (income up to $124,000) or 2% (up to $209,250), $10,000–$100,000, 20 years, first 18 months interest only; owner-occupied 1–4 units; about 45 days to close.
  • Cooling per year: window units 700 kWh, central AC 1,500 kWh; with a heat pump 1,200 kWh (geothermal 900), because a home without central AC usually starts cooling every room.
  • Oil defaults to last winter's average, $4.09 a gallon (this week: $6.15). Gas $2.60 a therm in winter. Propane $3.62.
  • Electric car: your miles and mpg, gasoline $4.39 a gallon (AAA, Sept 18, 2026); the EV goes 3.3 miles per kWh with 10% charging loss, charged at home; Off-Peak Charging credit about 3.7¢ per kWh on average. Car price not included.
  • Supplier: National Grid Basic Service 17.19¢ (Aug 2026–Jan 2027) or the town program 14.34¢ (until the Nov 2026 meter reads; the next price isn't posted).
  • Buy with neighbors: a company owns the geothermal, battery or solar for 5 years (the credit's recapture period) and claims the federal commercial credit, 30% for small projects plus a 10% bonus for U.S.-made content, on the cost after rebates. About half reaches the homeowner (credits sell at a discount and the company keeps a share). You keep paying about what you pay today (lease plus bills), then buy it back at year 5 for the balance left (cash, or over 15 years at your bank's rate) and own it. With solar sized to your use, what's left after that is the $10 connection fee. Solar only if running by Dec 31, 2027; batteries must meet the new foreign-supplier rules.
  • Electricity bills show supply and delivery separately. Delivery is 22.3¢ a kWh plus $10 a month whoever supplies you; the Heat Pump Rate lowers delivery Nov–Apr. Gas $2.60 a therm includes supply and delivery, plus $12.20 a month.
  • Today's prices, no inflation. Not counted: maintenance, resale value, a colder or warmer winter.
